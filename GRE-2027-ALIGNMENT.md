## Coverage and response behavior

ETS identifies three categories: **Text Completion**, **Sentence Equivalence** and **Reading Comprehension**. Wordtrail presents seven response subtypes under those categories, rather than seven separate official categories. Passage questions account for approximately half the Verbal measure; this is not a fixed per-section quota. [ETS Verbal overview](https://www.ets.org/gre/test-takers/general-test/prepare/content/verbal-reasoning.html)

| Category | Response subtype | Response required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Text Completion | One blank | One choice from five |
| Text Completion | Two blanks | One choice from three for each blank |
| Text Completion | Three blanks | One choice from three for each blank |
| Sentence Equivalence | Choose two answers | Two of six choices yielding equivalent completed sentences |
| Reading Comprehension | Select One Answer Choice | One choice from five |
| Reading Comprehension | Select One or More Answer Choices | Every correct choice among three; one, two or three may be correct |
| Reading Comprehension | Select-in-Passage | One sentence in the passage |

Text Completion and Sentence Equivalence require the whole response to be correct, with no partial credit. In Sentence Equivalence, each choice must fit the sentence and the resulting sentences must mean the same thing; isolated synonym matching is insufficient. [ETS Verbal response guidance](https://www.ets.org/gre/test-takers/general-test/prepare/content/verbal-reasoning.html) Reading's Select One or More Answer Choices also requires all correct choices and no incorrect ones. [ETS Reading Comprehension formats](https://grediagnostic.ets.org/GREDWeb/gred/reading_comprehension.jsp)

The interface presents each text-completion blank independently. Reading sentence selection uses whole passage sentences. Review explains the evidence, the tempting wrong answer and a reusable strategy. These learning interactions do not claim to replicate the exact ETS interface or question distribution.

## Practice flow and continuity

The original bank remains **99 questions**. One category chooser and session builder serve all new Verbal question entry points. Today's Text Completion shortcut selects one-blank practice. The builder reports the actual number of matching questions, explains reduced counts and provides recovery options for empty filters. Tab, category, subtype, difficulty, topic, requested length and unseen choices are remembered for the browser session.

Clear answer resets an editable response. The section map identifies complete, incomplete and marked items, and provides Next unanswered and Next marked controls. Answers remain editable within the current timed section until submission; checked drill answers are locked after feedback. Saved sessions can resume from the hub, while starting a replacement requires confirmation. A timed clock continues while the app is closed or in the background. Previously saved legacy sessions remain recoverable in their original runner.

Vocabulary support groups Meaning match, Saved words and Missed words separately from exam questions. Optional Issue writing is available through **Settings → Open writing studio**, with existing drafts and timers retained. Analytical Writing remains a separate task; moving the studio does not expand the Verbal bank.

## Scoring and a 170 Verbal goal

Verbal uses a 130–170 scale. The difficulty of the second Verbal section depends on first-section performance, and ETS equates scores across difficulty differences. Original app difficulty labels, percentage correct and XP do not establish an ETS-equivalent score. App simulations do not reproduce official adaptive routing. [ETS scoring explanation](https://www.ets.org/gre/test-takers/general-test/scores/understand-scores.html)

The preparation sequence in `targetScorePlan` is Wordtrail's recommendation: diagnose, repair vocabulary and reasoning gaps, review errors and lucky guesses, test transfer to unfamiliar questions, practice pacing, then benchmark with fresh official material. It supports an ambitious goal without guaranteeing 170. No universal “allowed mistakes for 170” threshold is asserted.

There is no deduction for an incorrect response. Use remaining time to answer rather than leave blanks. Mark-and-return behavior is meaningful only within the current section. [ETS test-day tips, page 20](https://www.ets.org/pdfs/gre/getting-into-business-school.pdf)
